# Regenerative economies and community-fund models: definitions, evidence and lessons for Compaki

Research date: 2026-10-25. Most sources were read through search-result excerpts and abstracts, not full papers. Figures marked "self-reported" or "promotional" come from the projects themselves. Figures marked "measured" come from transaction data, audited accounts or controlled studies.

## 1. What is a regenerative economy? (Raworth, Fullerton/Capital Institute, Ellen MacArthur, ReFi)

### Takeaway
"Regenerative economy" is a family of frameworks, not one measurable standard. The common idea is an economy that renews the social and natural capital it depends on instead of extracting it, and that keeps value circulating among stakeholders. Compaki can honestly claim to be "regenerative-inspired" or "circular at the community level" in the money-flow sense (value stays with and returns to participants). It should not claim ecological regeneration it does not measure.

### Cited Findings
- **Capital Institute (John Fullerton, 2015)** defines regenerative economics as applying "nature's laws and patterns of systemic health, self-organization, self-renewal, and regenerative vitality to socioeconomic systems." The *Regenerative Capitalism* white paper (April 2015) sets out 8 interconnected principles. Summaries list holistic wealth, empowered participation, resilient systems, the commons, and circulation of value among them. — [Capital Institute white paper](https://capitalinstitute.org/regenerative-capitalism/); [Executive Summary PDF](https://capitalinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/2015ExecSummary4-14-15.pdf); [INET podcast](https://www.ineteconomics.org/perspectives/podcasts/regenerative-economics-a-necessary-paradigm-shift-for-a-world-in-crisis)
- Capital Institute (Goerner) contrasts "regenerative" with "extractive" economies. It argues that wealth concentration and poor circulation make systems brittle, and that robust circulation among all parts of the system (likened to a body's blood flow) is a sign of health. — [Goerner, Regenerative vs Extractive Economies (2015)](https://capitalinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/000-Regenerative-Devel-Final-Goerner-Sept-1-2015.pdf)
- **Doughnut Economics (Kate Raworth):** first set out in the 2012 Oxfam paper "A safe and just space for humanity" and popularized by the 2017 book. The inner ring is a *social foundation* (minimum needs) and the outer ring an *ecological ceiling* (planetary boundaries). The goal is to meet everyone's needs within planetary limits, with economies that are "regenerative and distributive by design." — [Regenerative Economics textbook, 1.3.4](https://www.regenerativeeconomics.earth/regenerative-economics-textbook/1-introduction-to-the-economy/1-3-society-and-the-economy/1-3-4-doughnut-economics-model); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/doughnut-economics-4-questions-industry-leaders)
- **Circular economy (Ellen MacArthur Foundation):** three design-driven principles: (1) eliminate waste and pollution, (2) circulate products and materials at their highest value, (3) regenerate nature. — [EMF, Circular economy principles](https://www.ellenmacarthurfoundation.org/circular-economy-principles)
- **ReFi (regenerative finance) in web3:** Ethereum.org describes ReFi as tools and ideas built on blockchains "that aim to create economies which are regenerative, rather than extractive or exploitative." ReFi explicitly draws on Fullerton's 8 principles. Typical ReFi instruments are tokenized carbon credits, quadratic funding of public goods, community currencies and UBI tokens. — [ethereum.org/refi](https://ethereum.org/refi/); [CoinGecko explainer](https://www.coingecko.com/learn/what-is-regenerative-finance-refi-crypto)

### Inferences
- Compaki's fit with these frameworks is strongest on the *distributive and circulation* side: patronage-style "rewards" mode, a community fund, and value kept local. It is weakest on the *ecological* side: there is no material or nature metric. The pitch should say "regenerative/circular local economy: value recirculates to the community that creates it." It should not use "regenerative" in the environmental sense unless Compaki adds an ecological criterion, such as fundraise goals for local environmental projects.
- Fullerton's "robust circulation" principle maps directly to Compaki's automatic split on every sale. That is the most defensible conceptual link.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full list of Fullerton's 8 principles verbatim from the primary PDF. Commonly cited: right relationship; innovative, adaptive, responsive; comprehensively view wealth; empowered participation; honors community and place; edge effect abundance; robust circulatory flow; seeks balance. This list is from background knowledge and should be checked against the PDF before quoting.
- City-level Doughnut adoption (for example Amsterdam 2020) was not researched here, so there are no outcome figures.

## 2. Local multiplier effect evidence (NEF LM3, Civic Economics)

### Takeaway
There is consistent but methodologically limited evidence that money spent at locally owned businesses recirculates locally about 3 to 4 times more than money spent at chains. The usual figures are about 48% vs 14% for retail and about 79% vs 30% for restaurants. These are accounting estimates (business surveys and public records), not causal experiments. Cite them as "studies estimate."

### Cited Findings
- **Civic Economics, Salt Lake City (2012):** local independent retailers return about 52% of revenue to the local economy vs 14% for chains. Local restaurants recirculate about 79% vs 30% for chain restaurants. Method: independents opened their books, while chain values were estimated from public records. — [AMIBA, The Local Multiplier Effect](https://amiba.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/The-Local-Multiplier-Effect.pdf); [ILSR summary](https://archive.ilsr.org/?p=25357)
- The widely cited **"48% vs <14%"** is an *average across several Civic Economics studies* (Indie Impact Study Series with the American Booksellers Association, 2012, and earlier studies), not a single measurement. Attribution is inconsistent across secondary sources. — [AMIBA](https://amiba.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/The-Local-Multiplier-Effect.pdf); [ABA Bookweb](https://www.bookweb.org/node/25626)
- **Civic Economics, Dane County, Wisconsin (2022):** local independents return 47.4% of revenue to the county economy vs 13.6% for national chains. This is a recent replication of the pattern. — [Hngnews / DeForest Times](https://www.hngnews.com/deforest_times/news/business/survey-local-retailers-return-47-4-of-revenue-to-dane-county-economy/article_3427324a-8bd5-11ed-abbb-97486332dc37.html)
- **NEF LM3 (Local Multiplier 3):** a New Economics Foundation tool that tracks income through three rounds of spending in a defined area. Multiplier = (direct + indirect + induced) / direct. It was developed with Northumberland County Council and refined across all 26 North East England councils, covering over £3bn of annual spending and 140,000 suppliers. It is mainly used to inform local procurement. — [NEF Consulting LM3](https://www.nefconsulting.com/training-capacity-building/local-multiplier-3/); [NEF Consulting, Adam Wilkinson](https://www.nefconsulting.com/?p=11854)
- Academic critique of LM3: it is easy to use but sensitive to how "local" is defined, and it relies on self-reported supplier and staff locations. — [Mills, Univ. of Gloucestershire (2010)](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/3988/1/mills%202010.pdf) (critique point inferred from the paper's evaluative scope; full text not read)

### Inferences
- For Compaki, the defensible claim is: "Multi-vendor community marketplaces route spending to local independents, which studies estimate recirculate roughly 3x more revenue locally than chains (Civic Economics 2012–2022). On top of that, Compaki adds an explicit, auditable recirculation layer: the community fund."
- Compaki can do something these studies cannot: measure recirculation directly on-chain (share of fund payouts re-spent inside the marketplace, velocity, repeat buyers). That is a pitch differentiator: "LM3, but measured from on-chain data."

### Gaps
- I could not verify a specific LM3 headline figure (for example the often-quoted "£1 spent locally is worth £1.76" from NEF's 2002 *Plugging the Leaks* / Northumberland work) from a primary source in this session. Do not cite it without checking.

## 3. Community currencies and their measured results

### Takeaway
Results are mixed. The strongest *measured* successes are (a) WIR (Switzerland, since 1934), a counter-cyclical B2B credit network backed by decades of econometric evidence, (b) Sarafu/Grassroots Economics in Kenya, with large open transaction datasets and one RCT, and (c) Mumbuca in Maricá, Brazil, which scaled because the municipality funds it as basic income. Pure "buy local" paper currencies in rich countries (Bristol Pound and similar) got publicity but had little measurable economic impact and often closed when grant funding ran out.

### Cited Findings

**Sarafu / Grassroots Economics (Kenya)** — measured data, peer-reviewed
- *Scientific Data* (2022), Mattsson, Criscione & Ruddick, "Sarafu Community Inclusion Currency 2020–2021": open dataset of about 55,000 users and all transactions from 25 Jan 2020 to 15 Jun 2021 (first COVID year). It covers purchases, transfers, and savings and lending groups ("chamas"). Sarafu moved from paper community currencies to a feature-phone (USSD) interface starting in 2017. — [PMC full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9298170/); [CEU record](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/sarafu-community-inclusion-currency-20202021/)
- *Scientific Reports* (2023), Mattsson, Criscione & Takes, network analysis of circulation: circulation was **geographically concentrated (local)** and involved diverse users. **Local institutions (chamas, schools, shops) acted as hubs**, and **early adopters and women were pivotal** to circulation. — [Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek record "Circulation of a digital community currency"](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/2HGREJFAJUST5TWRFP5O2UB5HPMUZWQB?lang=en); [FRIBIS summary](https://fribis.uni-freiburg.de/?p=8471)
- A later Sarafu.Network dataset (2021–2023) covers about 20,000 pseudonymized users. — [CEU Open Research](https://openresearch.ceu.edu/items/8aef62fd-3e14-4b7c-ae9b-9317685ec347)
- **Kenya Red Cross COVID response (2020):** Kenya Red Cross adopted the Community Inclusion Currency (CIC) model. The Mukuru (Nairobi) pilot distributed 4,745,211 Sarafu (about US$43,688), and organizers report the full amount changed hands about **23 times on average**. This velocity figure is self-reported by Grassroots Economics. — [Grassroots Economics, Red Cross CIC pilot](https://grassecon.net/red-cross-cic.html); [VOA News](https://www.voanews.com/amp/5479243.html)
- **RCT (Mqamelo, 2021, *Frontiers in Blockchain*)**, described as possibly the first RCT on community currencies: a $30-equivalent CIC transfer was associated with a $93.51 increase in wallet balance and a $28.43 increase in spending on food and water. Effects differed by gender. — [Frontiers in Blockchain PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/blockchain/articles/10.3389/fbloc.2021.739751/pdf)
- **Bangla-Pesa (Mombasa, 2013), Sarafu's predecessor:** 109 businesses at launch, about €50/day traded. Self-reported survey: 83% of participants saw sales increase, averaging +22% of daily sales over baseline. The currency was briefly suspended when founders were arrested by Kenyan authorities (accused of forgery) and relaunched in Nov 2013 with government backing. — [IJCCR, Ruddick et al. 2015](https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9197); [Heinrich Böll Stiftung](https://www.boell.de/en/2016/01/25/how-bangla-pesa-tapped-value-informal-community); [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/the-crime-of-alleviating_b_3519858)
- Grassroots Economics itself says the single Sarafu token was meant as a short-term solution while software for community-issued tokens was delayed. — [Grassroots Economics, Sarafu Network update](https://www.grassrootseconomics.org/sarafu-network)

**Mumbuca, Maricá (Brazil)** — municipal basic income paid in a digital community currency
- Gama (CEDE/UFF), Feb 2018 to Aug 2020: about **R$201 million** spent in mumbucas, mostly from income-transfer beneficiaries. **Business recirculation (businesses re-spending mumbucas instead of converting to reais) was only 7%** over the period, rising to **26% during the pandemic months** (Apr–Aug 2020). Velocity was **1.79** over 31 months. Users reached **26% of the city's population** by Aug 2020. — [FRIBIS lecture abstract, Andrea Gama](https://www.fribis.uni-freiburg.de/event/andrea-gama-cede-local-currency-solidarity-economy-and-public-policy-the-case-of-the-mumbuca-economy-fribis-lecture-series/)
- A 2025 UFRN thesis finds coverage is wide but recirculation is limited because **businesses frequently convert mumbucas to reais**, with disparities across neighborhoods. — [UFRN repository](https://repositorio.ufrn.br/signposting/describedby/ab919c1b-514f-48b5-b8e3-91bb09dc2e3c)
- Diniz et al. (FGV, 2021) describe Maricá's program as one of the largest basic income programs during COVID-19, delivered through a digital community currency platform. — [FGV EAESP](https://eaesp.fgv.br/producao-intelectual/basic-income-digital-community-currency-digital-platforms-public-policies); [AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/globdev2021/8)
- A longitudinal UFF and Jain Family Institute evaluation is under way. I found no published results. — [EURAXESS posting](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/jobs/hosting/post-doctoral-researcher-evaluation-maric%C3%A1-basic-income-program-niter%C3%B3i-brazil); [Rest of World (2020)](https://restofworld.org/2020/your-money-is-no-good-in-marica/)

**Banco Palmas / Palmas (Fortaleza, Brazil)**
- Community development bank founded in Conjunto Palmeiras. It launched the "palmas" currency in 2003. By 2011, 270 businesses participated, with 46,000 palmas (about US$22,243) in circulation. — [Shareable](https://www.shareable.net/how-a-community-development-bank-in-brazil-stimulated-the-local-economy/)
- RAUSP (2012) case study reports "significant growth in local consumption" mainly via the palmas currency (qualitative, from bank documents 2011–2012). — [RAUSP / SciELO](https://scielo.br/j/rausp/a/5Rn56SGgjHZZPCbSH55X55F/?lang=en)
- FGV study on the move from paper palmas to e-money ("Palmas para o e-dinheiro"). — [FGV PDF](https://eaesp.fgv.br/sites/default/files/legacy/pesquisa-eaesp-files/arquivos/palmas_para_o_e-dinheiro.pdf)

**WIR Bank (Switzerland, since 1934)** — strongest long-run evidence
- Stodder (2009, *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*): WIR credit is **highly counter-cyclical**. Firms short of cash in recessions use more WIR, and WIR money is negatively correlated with GDP in the short run. Based on about 55 years of data in the 2005 version. The extra spending comes from new balances being created, not from faster velocity. — [Stodder, ISH 2011 conference paper](https://sdocument.ish-lyon.cnrs.fr/cc-conf/conferences.ish-lyon.cnrs.fr/index.php/cc-conf/2011/paper/view/107.html); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sce:scecf5:64)
- Stodder & Lietaer (2016, *Comparative Economic Studies*), "The Macro-Stability of Swiss WIR-Bank Credits." — [Lietaer.org PDF](https://bernard-lietaer.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/2016-The-Macro-Stability-of-Swiss-WIR-Bank-Credits-Balance-Velocity-and-Leverage-Stodder-Lietaer-annotated.pdf)
- WIR 2010 annual report: business volume of CHW 1.627 billion among WIR-account firms that year. — [WIR annual report 2010 (FR)](https://www.wir.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/Publikationen/Geschaeftsberichte/investor-relations-geschaeftsbericht-2010-fr.pdf)

**Bristol Pound (UK, 2012–2021)** — high profile, closed
- At its peak, more than £1m circulated per year through about 650 business members. — [Bristol247](https://www.bristol247.com/business/news-business/end-of-the-line-for-bristol-pound-after-nearly-a-decade/)
- Closure: the digital component stopped in Aug 2020 (accounts reverted to sterling at Bristol Credit Union). Paper notes were withdrawn in 2021, with about £40,000 still unspent. The CIC said it needed about £100,000 to cover **£13,000/month running costs**, i.e. it was not self-financing. — [Bristol247](https://www.bristol247.com/business/news-business/end-of-the-line-for-bristol-pound-after-nearly-a-decade/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_pound) (Wikipedia and 2021 news disagree on the paper withdrawal date, 2020 vs 2021)
- One estimate puts users at only about 0.02% of Bristol's population. — [Grokipedia/Bristol pound](https://grokipedia.com/page/Bristol_pound) (low-quality aggregator; use with caution)
- A literature review (JRFM, 2020) finds only about half of community currencies show any economic impact. It notes Bristol Pound and Ithaca Hours "lack evidence-based economic advantages" years after launch. — [Economic Advantages of Community Currencies, JRFM 13(11):271](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/jrfm13110271)

**Brixton Pound (UK)**
- Planned a digital relaunch on Algorand (date unclear in source). — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/76033/the-uks-local-currencies-are-going-crypto?amp=1)

**Chiemgauer (Bavaria, since 2003)** — longest-running demurrage currency
- Described as one of the most successful demurrage currencies since 1900. A 2017 study found turnover rose nearly 100-fold within a decade, and the share of currency hoarded (held out of circulation) fell from 90% to 29%. Undated sources cite about 5 million Chiemgauer spent per year (1 CH = €1). — [Wikipedia, Chiemgauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiemgauer); [Szeged journal article](https://ojs.bibl.u-szeged.hu/index.php/vikekkek/article/view/12430); [Deutsche Handwerks Zeitung](https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/?p=155531)

**Circles UBI (Gnosis) and impactMarket (Celo)** — web3 UBI
- Circles: each account mints 1 CRC per hour (personal-currency UBI with demurrage, trust graph). Circles 2.0 launched 21 May 2025 with 100,000+ people on the waitlist (Metri app). Total accounts are cited at about 200,000, but an independent review estimates about 10K *practical* users. Accounts vs active users differ by an order of magnitude. — [BusinessWire, May 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250521225718/en/Gnosis-Launches-Circles-2.0-A-Trust-Based-Digital-Currency-Where-Users-Issue-Their-Own-Tokens-Over-Time); [ResearchGate, UBI on blockchain: Circles](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378996450_Universal_basic_income_on_blockchain_the_case_of_circles_UBI); [Money2069 review](https://money2069.org/projects/circles)
- An earlier Berlin pilot of Circles struggled, while a Bali community group had better uptake (secondary blog, low reliability). — [Aurpay](https://aurpay.net/aurspace/circles-ubi/)
- impactMarket (Celo, pays UBI in cUSD stablecoin): about **US$3.07M distributed to 44,776 beneficiaries**, funded by 5,588 backers (undated ecosystem listing). It reported about $1.65M to about 25,000 beneficiaries in 25+ countries in early 2022. Raised $2.1M and launched the PACT token in Jan 2022. — [Celo PG ecosystem](https://www.celopg.eco/ecosystem/impact-market); [PR Newswire / WFMZ](https://www.wfmz.com/news/pr_newswire/pr_newswire_business/impactmarket-raises-2-1m-and-launches-pact-token-to-empower-millions-out-of-extreme-poverty/article_4c88239b-2cea-547d-b592-7b940f2b4c98.html)

### Inferences
- **Pattern of success:** the currencies that lasted or scaled had (1) an *anchor institution or funder that injects value* (Maricá's municipal budget, Red Cross, WIR's bank balance sheet), (2) a real *economic need* (cash scarcity in recessions, poverty, COVID), and (3) *dense local networks* (chamas, SMEs). "Buy local" identity currencies without an economic incentive (Bristol) stayed niche and died when grant money ended.
- **Convertibility trade-off:** Maricá shows that when businesses can convert to national currency, most do (7% recirculation outside crisis). Non-convertibility boosts local circulation but hurts merchant adoption. Compaki's design (sales in a convertible stable asset on Stellar, with the fund as a separate automatic split) avoids the merchant-adoption barrier. The cost is that Compaki *cannot claim* forced local recirculation. It can claim *incentivized* recirculation via rewards, and should measure it.
- The Sarafu finding that hubs (chamas, schools) and women drive circulation suggests Compaki should onboard anchor community institutions as early vendors or fund beneficiaries.

### Gaps
- No systematic survival or failure rate for community currencies was found. The JRFM review ("about half show economic impact") is the closest.
- Brixton Pound's current status (closed or digital) was not confirmed.
- Exact current Chiemgauer turnover (2023–2025) was not found.
- The figure "93% of Conjunto Palmeiras consumption is local" (often quoted for Banco Palmas) was not found in a primary source. Do not cite it.
- Up-to-date Sarafu Network 2024–2026 volume and user figures were not retrieved.

## 4. Cooperative models and patronage dividends (Mondragón, REI, consumer and platform co-ops)

### Takeaway
Patronage refunds (surplus returned to members in proportion to how much they buy or sell) are a 150-year-old, proven model at very large scale. The top 300 co-ops turned over US$2.79tn in 2023. But they work as *a share of surplus that the business can afford*, not as a guarantee. REI shows that dividends compete with profitability in bad years. Platform co-ops (Stocksy) prove the model can work digitally at small scale. Compaki's "rewards" mode is a programmable patronage refund.

### Cited Findings
- **ICA World Cooperative Monitor (Nov 2025 edition, 2023 data):** the top 300 co-ops and mutuals had a combined turnover of **US$2.79 trillion**. Leaders include Crédit Agricole, State Farm and REWE. The Jan 2024 edition (2021 data) reported about US$2.4tn. — [ICA press release (Nov 2025)](https://ica.coop/sites/default/files/2025-11/pr_world_cooperative_monitor_en.pdf); [Co-op News](https://www.thenews.coop/worlds-top-300-co-ops-and-mutuals-have-combined-turnover-of-us2-79tn/); [ICA-AP, WCM 2023](https://icaroap.icaap.coop/icanews/world-cooperative-monitor-2023)
- **Mondragón (Basque Country):** 2024 sales of **€11.213bn** (+1.6%), EBITDA €1.661bn, net profit €632m, about 70,085 workers across 81 self-governing co-ops and 12 R&D centres. Eroski (consumer/retail co-op) had €6.193bn in sales. Wikipedia lists 2025 revenue of €11.322bn and 71,415 employees. — [Co-op News](https://www.thenews.coop/mondragon-worker-co-op-federation-reports-sales-of-e11-213bn/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mondragon_Corporation)
- **REI Co-op (US consumer co-op):** the member dividend was renamed the "Co-op Member Reward" in 2021 (typically about 10% of eligible purchases, "not guaranteed"). It can be a patronage dividend, a promotional reward, or a mix, decided yearly. — [REI membership](https://www.rei.com/membership)
- REI's financial strain: **net loss of $311M in 2023** (revenue $3.76bn, −2.4%) after a $165M loss in 2022. Causes cited include higher hourly pay, *member rewards*, and a $169M tax valuation allowance. Layoffs followed in 2023 (167), Jan 2024 (357) and Jan 2025 (428, ending its Experiences business). The CEO projected near break-even operating income for 2024 *excluding the member dividend*. — [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/rei-revenue-income-fall-2023-expand-texas); [Bicycle Retailer](https://bicycleretailer.com/industry-news/2024/05/01/reis-net-losses-grow-revenues-decline-2); [ADN/Seattle Times, Jan 2025](https://www.adn.com/business-economy/2025/01/08/struggling-rei-cuts-hundreds-of-employees-axes-tour-and-events-business/)
- **Stocksy United (stock-photo platform co-op, Canada, founded 2013):** multi-stakeholder co-op (founders, staff and artists as member classes, one member one vote). Artists receive **50–75% of each license** vs an industry benchmark of about 20%. Revenue was $7.9M in 2015 (+126%) and about $10.7M in 2017. It paid $5.7M in royalties and **$300K in patronage dividends** in 2017, and its first $200K dividend on 2015 results. It reports more than $50.1M in cumulative royalties. — [Wikipedia, Stocksy United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocksy_United); [BrainStation](https://brainstation.io/magazine/stocksy-united-doubles-revenue); [Selling Stock](https://www.selling-stock.com/Article/stocksy-united-removes-membership-cap)

### Inferences
- **Defensible framing:** "Compaki's rewards mode is a patronage refund (the mechanism REI, consumer co-ops and Stocksy use), made automatic and transparent by a smart contract." Patronage refunds are proven at scale. The new part is per-transaction, real-time, auditable accounting.
- **Key design lesson from REI:** a fixed-percentage dividend can strain finances. Compaki's model takes a *fixed % of each sale into the fund* (not a promise funded from profit), so the payout is always fully funded. That is structurally safer. It should still state clearly that rewards are variable and depend on volume.
- Stocksy shows platform co-ops can succeed in niches with high-skill, scarce suppliers. It does not prove the model at mass-marketplace scale.

### Gaps
- Fairmondo (German co-op marketplace, an eBay/Amazon alternative) was not researched successfully. No results returned. From background knowledge it has remained small, but there is no source here.
- Platform Cooperativism Consortium census numbers (number of platform co-ops, survival rates) were not retrieved.
- REI's audited 2024 and 2025 results were not found.

## 5. Crowdfunding and community-fundraise outcomes, and ReFi projects that failed or faded

### Takeaway
On-chain community funding has moved real money: Gitcoin has distributed about $50–60M+ since 2019 through quadratic funding. But it depends on matching pools from foundations, and Gitcoin itself wound down its software arm in 2025. The best-known ReFi failure (KlimaDAO/Toucan, 2021–2022) collapsed because of speculative token design, low-quality underlying assets, and dependence on an off-chain registry's rules. Compaki should avoid a speculative governance token and keep the fund in a stable asset.

### Cited Findings
- **Gitcoin Grants:** over **$50M distributed through quadratic funding** (official Gitcoin blog). $50.82M across the first 15 rounds (GR15 alone $4.4M). A third-party directory cites **over $60M since 2019** (undated). Round 1 (Feb 2019) had a $25K matching pool and $13,242 from 126 donors. Later rounds were much smaller: GG22 (Aug 2024) about $1.5M+ and GG23 (Dec 2024) about $1.2M across 235 projects. — [Gitcoin community page](https://community.gitcoin.co/gitcoin-initiatives/gitcoin-grants); [GG22 results](https://explore.gitcoin.co/campaigns/gg22-results); [Grants Stack listing](https://explore.gitcoin.co/apps/gitcoin-grants-stack)
- In **April 2025 Gitcoin shut down Gitcoin Labs** and sunset Grants Stack and the Allo protocol, citing no realistic path to profitability. It refocused on a grants program planned at about $4.29M across 3 rounds in 2025. — [The Block, Apr 2025](https://www.theblock.co/post/352055/ethereum-public-goods-funding-protocol-gitcoin-winding-down-its-software-division); [Gitcoin 2025 strategy](https://gitcoin.co/blog/gitcoin-grants-2025-strategy)
- Quadratic funding weights the *number of contributors* over the size of contributions ("more aligned with the sentiment of the community"). — [Gitcoin community page](https://community.gitcoin.co/gitcoin-initiatives/gitcoin-grants)
- **KlimaDAO / Toucan (carbon ReFi):** the KLIMA token fell from about $3,681 (end of Oct 2021) to about $4.89, and Toucan's BCT carbon token fell from about $8.31 (Nov 2021) to about $2.14. In **May 2022 Verra banned tokenization of retired credits**, sending BCT to new lows. Critics (CarbonPlan) said the bridged credits were old and low quality. KlimaDAO's treasury was backed by BCT (14.35 BCT per KLIMA in Dec 2021), so it inherited that risk. — [S&P Global, May 2022](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/052522-as-verra-halts-tokenization-of-carbon-credits-toucan-vows-to-keep-web3-ethos-alive); [QCIntel](https://qcintel.com/carbon/article/verra-bans-credit-tokenisation-sending-toucan-bct-to-new-low-6182.html); [Redd Monitor](https://reddmonitor.substack.com/p/verra-prohibits-tokenisation-of-retired); [S&P Global, Dec 2021](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/121421-bct-carbon-credits-issued-sold-into-klima-dao-in-dec-despite-no-arbitrage)
- A 2026 secondary source claims KLIMA traded at about $0.04 by April 2026 (about −99.99% from peak) and that the DAO rebranded as "Klima Protocol." Not verified against primary sources. — [Plisio ReFi explainer (ES)](https://plisio.net/es/defi/what-is-regenerative-finance-refi)

### Inferences
- **Lessons for Compaki's fundraise mode:** (1) fund in a stablecoin (USDC/EURC on Stellar), not a speculative token. Every failure above involved token-price collapse. (2) Tie the fund to *real economic activity* (sales), not to emissions or token incentives. (3) Avoid dependence on off-chain registries or rule-makers that can invalidate the asset. (4) Gitcoin shows community matching attracts many small donors but depends on external sponsors. Compaki's sale-generated fund is a self-financing alternative to sponsor-funded matching pools.
- Gitcoin's pivot is also a warning about sustainability: infrastructure for community funding needs a revenue model. Compaki has one, a platform fee separate from the community fund.

### Gaps
- Giveth totals (donations, GIVbacks) were not retrieved.
- No independent impact evaluation of Gitcoin-funded projects (outcomes vs dollars) was found.
- No data on reward-based or donation crowdfunding success rates (for example Kickstarter's success rate) was collected here.

## 6. Lessons: why community currencies fail, and implications for Compaki's design

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are low adoption and network effects, lack of liquidity or convertibility for merchants, dependence on grants with no running-cost model, regulatory friction, and speculative token design. Compaki's architecture addresses most of them: sales in a convertible stable asset, an automatic on-chain split, transparent rules, and a fund financed by trade rather than donors. Its main remaining risks are reaching marketplace liquidity (vendors and buyers) and showing that rewards actually change behavior.

### Cited Findings
- **Adoption and impact are the main problem:** only about half of community currencies have measurable economic impact, and only about a third give access to goods that would otherwise be unaffordable. Bristol and Ithaca Hours lacked evidence of economic advantages. — [JRFM 2020 review](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/jrfm13110271)
- **Operating costs and grant dependence:** Bristol Pound needed about £100K to cover £13K/month costs and closed. — [Bristol247](https://www.bristol247.com/business/news-business/end-of-the-line-for-bristol-pound-after-nearly-a-decade/)
- **Convertibility leaks:** Maricá merchants convert mumbucas to reais, so business recirculation was only 7% (26% in crisis). — [FRIBIS/Gama](https://www.fribis.uni-freiburg.de/event/andrea-gama-cede-local-currency-solidarity-economy-and-public-policy-the-case-of-the-mumbuca-economy-fribis-lecture-series/); [UFRN 2025](https://repositorio.ufrn.br/signposting/describedby/ab919c1b-514f-48b5-b8e3-91bb09dc2e3c)
- **Regulatory risk:** Bangla-Pesa founders were arrested in 2013 on forgery charges before the currency was legitimized. — [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/the-crime-of-alleviating_b_3519858); [IJCCR](https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9197)
- **Value injection works:** the largest-scale cases (Maricá basic income, Red Cross CIC, impactMarket) involve an external funder injecting money. — [FGV](https://eaesp.fgv.br/producao-intelectual/basic-income-digital-community-currency-digital-platforms-public-policies); [Grassroots Economics](https://grassecon.net/red-cross-cic.html); [Celo PG](https://www.celopg.eco/ecosystem/impact-market)
- **Networks and hubs matter:** Sarafu circulation is local and hub-driven (community institutions, women, early adopters). — [Mattsson et al. 2023 record](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/2HGREJFAJUST5TWRFP5O2UB5HPMUZWQB?lang=en)
- **Demurrage and hoarding:** Chiemgauer's demurrage cut hoarding from 90% to 29% of circulation. — [Wikipedia/Chiemgauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiemgauer)
- **Counter-cyclicality is real value:** WIR credit expands when the economy shrinks. — [Stodder](https://sdocument.ish-lyon.cnrs.fr/cc-conf/conferences.ish-lyon.cnrs.fr/index.php/cc-conf/2011/paper/view/107.html)
- **Speculative tokens collapse:** KLIMA and BCT lost most of their value within months. — [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/energy-transition/052522-as-verra-halts-tokenization-of-carbon-credits-toucan-vows-to-keep-web3-ethos-alive)
- **Fixed dividend promises strain finances:** REI's member rewards were among the cited causes of its 2023 loss. — [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/rei-revenue-income-fall-2023-expand-texas)

### Inferences (design implications for Compaki)
1. **Convertible stable asset (not a new currency):** this avoids the merchant-liquidity and convertibility barrier that killed adoption elsewhere. The trade-off is that Compaki cannot force local recirculation. Mitigation: optionally pay rewards as marketplace credit, or with a bonus if re-spent locally, which is a soft version of Chiemgauer's demurrage and Maricá's closed loop.
2. **Automatic split per sale = self-financing fund:** unlike Bristol (grant-funded) or Gitcoin (sponsor-funded matching), the fund grows with trade. Pitch claim: "No donor dependence: the fund is financed by the community's own trade."
3. **Rewards = programmable patronage refund:** this is a proven co-op mechanism (REI, Stocksy, consumer co-ops; the top 300 co-ops turn over US$2.79tn). Because it is a % of each sale rather than a promise from profits, it cannot become an unfunded liability (the REI lesson). Say "variable, proportional to participation."
4. **Transparency as the edge over legacy models:** on-chain accounting lets Compaki publish recirculation metrics (fund inflows, payouts, re-spend rate, velocity, share of local vendors). That turns the soft LM3/Civic Economics claims into measured ones. Suggested KPI set: % of reward payouts re-spent within the marketplace within 30 days; transactions per unit of fund (velocity); number of repeat buyers; fundraise goals reached.
5. **Seed with anchor institutions:** onboard co-ops, schools, NGOs and markets as vendors and fundraise beneficiaries (Sarafu hub lesson). Consider an external "value injection" partner (municipality, NGO, foundation) to top up the fund, following the Maricá and Red Cross pattern.
6. **No speculative token:** keep governance off a tradable token, or use a non-transferable reputation instead (KlimaDAO lesson).
7. **Regulatory clarity:** use regulated stablecoins and SEP-compliant anchors on Stellar. Avoid creating a parallel "currency" that could clash with central-bank rules (Bangla-Pesa lesson).
8. **Honest claims for the pitch:**
   - SAFE: "Local independents recirculate an estimated ~3x more revenue locally than chains (Civic Economics, 2012–2022)." "Patronage refunds are a proven cooperative mechanism at global scale." "Digital community currencies in Kenya circulated locally through community hubs (peer-reviewed, Sci. Reports 2023)." "WIR has stabilized Swiss SMEs for 90 years."
   - AVOID: "community currencies multiply money 23x" (self-reported, one pilot); "93% of spending stays local" (unverified); "regenerative" in the ecological sense without metrics; any return-on-investment promise for rewards.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found on whether *patronage-style rewards* in a *digital marketplace* change buyer or vendor behavior (retention, basket size). Compaki would need to generate that evidence itself (a pilot with an A/B test is a credible "next step" for the pitch).
- No data was found on community-fund "fundraise" modes embedded in marketplaces (for example round-up-at-checkout charity programs), which would be the nearest comparable for Compaki's fundraise mode.
