# Market size and usage of online stores, store builders and marketplaces (global + LatAm), for Compaki TAM/SAM/SOM

Research date: 2026-10-25. Each figure gives the year it covers. Labels: **[Reported]** means a company filing or official release. **[Estimate]** means a forecast or an analyst estimate. **[Vendor]** means a market-research seller or a marketing blog, which is lower confidence. **[Secondhand]** means the figure was seen only through an aggregator or the press and not checked against the original.

## 1. Merchants and stores on Shopify, WooCommerce, Wix, Squarespace, Tiendanube/Nuvemshop and Mercado Shops (and what happened to Mercado Shops)

### Takeaway
Store Leads counted about 14.2M live online stores in October 2026. WooCommerce has about 4.3M of them and Shopify about 3.2M. Tiendanube/Nuvemshop is the LatAm leader with about 208K live stores and about R$15B GMV in 2025. Shopify processed US$378B GMV in 2025 and no longer publishes an exact merchant count. Its filing only says "millions". Mercado Libre shut Mercado Shops on 31 Dec 2025 and moved sellers to "Mi Página", which lives inside the marketplace. That left a gap for independent, community-branded storefronts in LatAm.

### Cited Findings
- **Shopify GMV 2025: US$378.4B, up 29% YoY. Revenue 2025: US$11.6B, up 30%.** [Reported, 10-K FY2025] — [Shopify 10-K FY2025 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480526000007/shop-20251231.htm); exact figure of US$378.44B vs US$292.28B in 2024 — [Digital Commerce 360, Feb 2026](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/02/17/shopify-revenue-gmv-q4-2025/)
- Shopify Q4 2025 GMV: US$123.84B, its first quarter above US$100B. B2B GMV grew 96% in 2025. Net income 2025: US$1.23B. FCF: about US$2B. [Reported, via press] — [Retail Insight Network / Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/shopify-posts-30-revenue-growth-113122526.html)
- Shopify's merchant count is not disclosed as a number. The 10-K says it has "millions of merchants from more than 175 countries". [Reported] — [Shopify 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480526000007/shop-20251231.htm)

  - Squarespace: **391,440**
  - Tiendanube/Nuvemshop: **208,044**
  - BigCommerce: 36,333
  - VTEX: 5,923

The WooCommerce plugin page claims 7M+ active installs. Store counts therefore depend on method, and Store Leads is the most cited. [Secondhand] — [Visionary Marketing, May 2026](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/ecommerce-platform-market-share-2026)
- **Tiendanube/Nuvemshop:**
  - Company says it has "more than 180,000 brands" in five countries. [Company claim] — [Mercado & Consumo, Jun 2026](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/22/06/2026/ecommerce/nuvemshop-mira-r-20-bilhoes-em-vendas-de-parceiros-e-mantem-ipo-no-radar/)
  - Press reports **GMV of about R$15B in 2025**, with a target of R$20B in 2026. [Secondhand / company statement to press] — same source
  - The company's NuvemCommerce 2026 report cites "more than R$6.5B" in sales, up 35% vs 2024. This looks like a Brazil-only or sample-only figure and does not match the R$15B platform GMV, so the scope is unclear.   - H1 2026: store sales up 44% YoY, with 14.5M orders. [Company, via press] — [GiroNews](https://gironews.com/varejo-digital/e-commerce-cresce-44-no-primeiro-semestre-aponta-nuvemshop/)
  - Store Leads separately counted 176,933 live Tiendanube stores in March 2026. — [Store Leads Tiendanube report](https://storeleads.app/reports/tiendanube)
- **Mercado Shops was discontinued effective 31 December 2025.** It had let sellers run their own storefronts on top of Mercado Libre's logistics, ads and payments. In January 2025 MELI said sellers would move to "Mi Página", described as similar functionality "fully embedded within our Marketplace (without an external storefront)". [Reported, 10-K FY2025] — [MercadoLibre 10-K FY2025 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1099590/000109959026000006/meli-20251231.htm)
- A third-party seller tool says that after the change, stores on their own domains stopped working, links that were not migrated broke, and Mi Página has a monthly cost and depends fully on the MELI ecosystem. [Vendor blog, Mexico] — [UpSeller](https://upseller.com/es/blog-article-561)

### Inferences
- About 14M live stores worldwide means tens of millions of merchants and creators already know the store-builder model. Compaki's difference is a multi-vendor community storefront with split payouts, not a single-merchant store.
- The Mercado Shops closure is a useful pitch point. LatAm's biggest commerce player dropped independent storefronts and pulled sellers back into its marketplace. Sellers who want their own branded or community channel now have fewer options, mainly Tiendanube and Shopify, and both serve a single merchant.
- Tiendanube's GMV converts to roughly US$2.7–2.9B in 2025, assuming R$5.2–5.6 per US$. That is my conversion and not a reported USD figure. It is a useful benchmark for the long tail of LatAm independent sellers.

### Gaps
- Shopify no longer publishes an exact merchant count. Older public figures such as "millions" or ">5M" are not in the FY2025 filing.
- No official Wix or Squarespace count of commerce-specific merchants for 2025–26 was found. Wix and Squarespace report total sites and subscriptions, which include non-commerce sites.
- No official number was found for how many Mercado Shops stores existed at shutdown.

## 2. Digital buyers worldwide and in LatAm; LatAm e-commerce size and growth

### Takeaway
About 2.8–3.0B people buy online worldwide (2025 estimates). LatAm retail e-commerce is about US$191B in 2025, growing 12.2%, the fastest of any region (eMarketer). Mercado Libre alone had 121M unique buyers and US$65B GMV in 2025. Mexico had 77.2M digital buyers and MX$941B in retail e-commerce in 2025 (AMVO). PCMI's broader all-transactions definition puts LatAm e-commerce at about US$769B in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Digital buyers worldwide in 2025:**
  - eMarketer's chart title says ">3 billion digital buyers worldwide in 2025". [Estimate] — [eMarketer chart, Feb 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/270796/will-more-than-3-billion-digital-buyers-worldwide-2025)
  - Statista is quoted at 2.77B for 2025 and 2.71B for 2024. [Estimate, Secondhand] — [Envisage Digital, citing Statista](https://www.envisagedigital.co.uk/ecommerce-statistics/)
  - The two use different definitions.
- **LatAm retail e-commerce 2025: about US$191.25B, up 12.2%, the fastest-growing region.** eMarketer says this is about 1.5x the global growth rate. [Estimate] — [eMarketer LatAm Ecommerce Forecast 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/latin-america-ecommerce-forecast-2025); the USD figure via [Mundo do Marketing](https://mundodomarketing.com.br/e-commerce-na-america-latina-cresce-12-2-e-lidera-ritmo-global-em-2025)
- eMarketer expects more than US$150B in additional LatAm e-commerce sales over the next two years, and says over two-thirds of online sales sit outside the leading players. [Estimate] — [eMarketer LatAm Ecommerce Market Shares 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/latin-america-ecommerce-market-shares-2025)
- **LatAm digital buyers: 293.2M**, from eMarketer's 2024 report. No 2025 figure was found. [Estimate, Secondhand via search snippet] — [eMarketer LatAm forecast](https://www.emarketer.com/content/latin-america-ecommerce-forecast-2025)
- Endeavor and Mercado Libre project about US$215.3B in LatAm online shopping for 2025. Argentina, Brazil and Mexico make up about 85% of it. [Estimate; MELI is an interested party] — [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/latin-american-e-commerce-top-202252635.html)
- **PCMI puts LatAm e-commerce at about US$769B in 2025, up 21%.** This broad definition includes services and digital goods. Account-to-account payments (Pix, Bre-B) are growing about twice as fast as cards. [Estimate, Secondhand via AMI, PCMI's sister firm] — [Americas Market Intelligence](https://americasmi.com/insights/top-selling-products-latin-america/)
- **Mercado Libre FY2025** [Reported, Q4 2025 earnings release, 24 Feb 2026]:
  - GMV: **US$65.0B** (FY2024: US$51.5B)
  - Unique active buyers: **121M** for the year, 83M in Q4
  - Items sold: 2.43B
  - Total payment volume (TPV): US$277.8B
  - Fintech monthly active users: 78M
  - Net revenue and financial income: US$28.9B, up 39%
  - Q4 GMV: US$19.9B, up 36.8%
  - Note: from Q2 2025 the definitions include food delivery.
  - Sources: [MELI 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/sec/meli-8-k-2026-02-24-0001099590-26-000003/meli-20260224xex991.htm); [Business Wire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224265595/en/Mercado-Libre-Caps-Stellar-2025-Performance-with-45-YoY-Revenue-Growth-in-Q4-as-Strategic-Investments-Accelerate-Market-Share-Gains)
- **Mexico (AMVO Estudio de Venta Online 2026, covering 2025)** [Industry association]:
  - Retail e-commerce: **MX$941B**, up 19.2%
  - E-commerce is **17.7%** of retail sales
  - **77.2M digital buyers**, up from about 67M in 2024
  - Sources: [AMVO press PDF](https://images.produ.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/12132700/AMVO_Estudio-de-Venta-Online-2026-Prensa-1_compressed.pdf); [Marketing4eCommerce MX](https://marketing4ecommerce.mx/estudio-de-venta-online-2026-amvo-mexico/)

### Inferences
- Comparing MELI's US$65B GMV with eMarketer's US$191B gives MELI about a third of LatAm retail e-commerce. The comparison is rough because the definitions differ (MELI now includes food delivery). It still shows that LatAm online buying is concentrated in one marketplace, which lets Compaki pitch "community-owned alternatives".
- Use the eMarketer retail figure (US$191B) as the conservative LatAm anchor. Do not use PCMI's US$769B on a product-sales slide, because it includes services and travel.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 LatAm digital-buyer total was found from a primary source. Only the 2024 figure of 293.2M is available, through eMarketer.
- Statista's LatAm buyer page is paywalled and was not verified.

## 3. Global e-commerce size and the share sold through marketplaces

### Takeaway
Global retail e-commerce is about US$6.4T in 2025 and about US$6.9T in 2026, roughly 20% of retail (eMarketer). Marketplaces handle most of it. The top 100 marketplaces had about US$3.83T GMV in 2024 (Digital Commerce 360). ECDB estimates that third-party marketplaces are 83.4% of global e-commerce revenue in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Worldwide retail e-commerce:**
  - **US$6.419T in 2025, up 6.8%**
  - **US$6.88T in 2026**
  - US$7.375T in 2027
  - [Estimate, eMarketer Feb 2025; Secondhand] — [Shopify blog citing eMarketer](https://www.shopify.com/blog/global-ecommerce-sales); [Review42 compilation](https://resources.review42.com/ecommerce-statistics/)
- E-commerce is about 20.5% of global retail in 2025, rising to about 22.5% by 2028. [Estimate, Secondhand] — [Fungies compilation](https://fungies.io/ecommerce-statistics-2026-3/)
- **The top 100 online marketplaces had about US$3.832T GMV in 2024, up 10% from 2023.** Digital Commerce 360's 2025 database ranks marketplaces by 2024 third-party GMV, and the ranked platforms' projected 2025 third-party sales range from US$26.4B to US$803.3B. [Estimate, DC360] — [Digital Commerce 360, top marketplaces](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/infographic-top-online-marketplaces/); [DC360 Marketplaces Database](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/product/online-marketplaces-database/)
- **Third-party marketplaces' share of global e-commerce revenue rose from 81.0% in 2023 to 83.4% in 2025** (ECDB). Europe is at 60.8% and Asia at 97.0%, driven by China. [Estimate] — [TYN Magazine, citing ECDB](https://tynmagazine.com/marketplaces-now-account-for-83-4-of-global-e-commerce-revenues/)
- Amazon (about US$845B), Pinduoduo (about US$817B) and Douyin (about US$648B) are estimated at about 36% of global e-commerce together. [Secondhand, unverified] — [Review42](https://resources.review42.com/ecommerce-statistics/)

### Inferences
- A global marketplace TAM between US$3.8T (DC360 top-100 GMV in 2024, conservative) and about US$5.3T (6.42T × 83.4%, mixing eMarketer and ECDB, which I calculated) is defensible. The pitch should say which one it uses.
- The 83.4% share is inflated by Asia's 97%, where super-apps and social commerce dominate. The share outside Asia is much lower, around 60% in Europe.

### Gaps
- Digital Commerce 360's 2025 top-100 total and a LatAm-specific marketplace share are behind a paywall and were not found.

## 4. Market size of multi-vendor marketplace software, community/social commerce, creator economy, handicrafts, and LatAm MSMEs

### Takeaway
These markets are estimated mostly by market-research vendors, with wide ranges:
- Marketplace platform software: about US$12.4B in 2025.
- LatAm e-commerce platform software: about US$0.68B in 2025.
- Creator economy: about US$190–255B in 2025.
- LatAm social commerce: about US$14.6B in 2025, up 20%.
- Global handicrafts: about US$790–990B in 2025.

LatAm MSMEs are about 99% of firms and employ about two-thirds of workers, and about half of workers were informal in 2022. No reliable count of LatAm artisans was found.

### Inferences
- "Community commerce" has no standard market-size category. The closest proxies are social commerce (LatAm US$14.6B) and the long tail of independent stores (Tiendanube about US$2.7–2.9B GMV). Compaki should say plainly that its SAM is built from these proxies.
- The high informality (about 49%) and MSME dominance (99% of firms) support the problem slide. Producers are many and fragmented, they lack formal payment or storefront tools, and they cannot easily split income in group or cooperative settings.
- Handicraft "market sizes" of US$0.8–1T are vendor estimates that include offline retail and mass-produced decor. Use them only as color, not as TAM.

### Gaps
- No absolute count of LatAm MSMEs was found from CEPAL, IDB or the World Bank. Figures often cited elsewhere are in the tens of millions of units including informal ones, but none was verified here.
- No reliable LatAm artisan headcount was found. ILO, UNESCO or national bodies (e.g. FONART Mexico, Artesanías de Colombia) would be needed.
- No size was found for the cooperative-commerce market.
- No global social-commerce figure from a primary source was found.

## 5. A defensible TAM/SAM/SOM framing for Compaki

### Takeaway
Recommended framing:
- **TAM** is global marketplace GMV, about US$3.8T (DC360 top-100 GMV, 2024) to US$5.3T (2025, derived).
- **Regional TAM** is LatAm retail e-commerce, US$191B in 2025.
- **SAM** is LatAm community, social and independent small-seller commerce, about US$15–20B in 2025 (social commerce US$14.6B plus independent store-builder GMV of about US$3B).
- **SOM** at three years is a bottom-up target in the low millions of US$ GMV. At a 2% take rate that is about US$50–200K in platform revenue, which is honest at Ideathon stage.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- Global retail e-commerce: US$6.42T in 2025 and US$6.88T in 2026 — [eMarketer via Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/global-ecommerce-sales)
- Marketplaces' share: 83.4% in 2025 — [ECDB via TYN](https://tynmagazine.com/marketplaces-now-account-for-83-4-of-global-e-commerce-revenues/). Top-100 marketplace GMV: US$3.83T in 2024 — [DC360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/infographic-top-online-marketplaces/)
- LatAm retail e-commerce: US$191.25B in 2025, up 12.2% — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/latin-america-ecommerce-forecast-2025)
- LatAm social commerce: US$14.62B in 2025 — [ResearchAndMarkets](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250514165682/en/Latin-America-Social-Commerce-Intelligence-Report-2025-Market-to-Grow-by-20.1-to-Reach-%2414.62-Billion-this-Year---Future-Growth-Dynamics-to-2030---ResearchAndMarkets.com)
- Tiendanube GMV: about R$15B in 2025, with about 180–208K stores — [Mercado & Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/22/06/2026/ecommerce/nuvemshop-mira-r-20-bilhoes-em-vendas-de-parceiros-e-mantem-ipo-no-radar/); [Store Leads](https://www.storeleads.app/reports)
- Mercado Libre GMV: US$65.0B, with 121M buyers in 2025 — [MELI 8-K](https://www.boardroomalpha.com/sec/meli-8-k-2026-02-24-0001099590-26-000003/meli-20260224xex991.htm)
- Mercado Shops shut on 31 Dec 2025 — [MELI 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1099590/000109959026000006/meli-20251231.htm)
- LatAm MSMEs: 99% of firms and about two-thirds of employment — [IDB Invest 2026](https://idbinvest.org/en/download/27250)

### Inferences (proposed framing; my own calculations and assumptions, marked)
- **TAM, global marketplace GMV:**
  - US$3.8T is conservative (DC360, 2024).
  - About US$5.3T is derived: 6.42T × 83.4% (2025).
  - At Compaki's 2% platform fee, that implies a theoretical revenue pool of about US$76–107B. This is illustrative only.
- **Regional TAM, LatAm retail e-commerce:** US$191B in 2025, the fastest-growing region at 12.2%. MELI's roughly one-third share shows how concentrated it is.
- **SAM, LatAm community and small-producer commerce:** about US$15–20B in 2025.
  - It is built from LatAm social commerce (US$14.6B) plus independent store-builder GMV (Tiendanube about US$2.7–2.9B; the share of Shopify and WooCommerce in LatAm is unknown).
  - The rationale is that this is the trade that happens outside the big marketplaces, through WhatsApp, Instagram, fairs and small storefronts. That is where community and cooperative sellers operate.
  - At a 2% fee, this is a revenue pool of about US$300–400M.
- **SOM, three years, bottom-up.** These are illustrative assumptions, not data, and should be presented as targets:
  - 300 community marketplaces × 20 vendors × US$400 GMV per vendor per month gives about US$2.9M GMV per year. At 2%, that is about US$58K in platform revenue.
  - A stretch case of 1,000 marketplaces × 25 vendors × US$500 per month gives about US$150M GMV per year. That is about 0.75–1% of the SAM, and about US$3M in platform revenue.
  - Benchmark: Tiendanube reached about 180K+ stores after more than 10 years, which puts the scale of "reachable" in context.
- **Problem-slide numbers to pair with this:**
  - 49% informal employment and 99% MSME share of firms.
  - The largest LatAm player (MELI) removed independent storefronts at the end of 2025.
  - Payouts that must be split among several parties (vendor, organizer, community fund) are not served natively by single-merchant builders such as Shopify, WooCommerce and Tiendanube.

### Gaps
- No dataset was found that sizes "community/cooperative marketplaces" directly, so the SAM depends on proxies.
- The LatAm share of Shopify and WooCommerce GMV was not found, so the SAM's independent-store component is understated.
- The SOM figures are assumptions. Validate them with pilot data such as the vendors per community and the ticket size of target groups.
